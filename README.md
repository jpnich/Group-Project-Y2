# Smart Electrical Meter for Testbed Simulation
The task was to design a smart meter that could interface between a number of inputs (representing power sources) and outputs (representing loads, i.e., hospitals, residential blocks, etc) for a simulated electrical grid, represented by a test bed. The device revolved around 4 distinct areas: housing (mechanical stream), interface / signal conditioning (analogue/mixed-signal electronics strean), control system and microcontroller operation (software stream), and assembly (integration stream). The team was divided up accordingly.

My contribution to the project included being the team leader and analogue electronics specialist. This allowed me to go through a full design process for implementing the interface electronics that would enable the input and output signals to successfully communicate with the microcontroller and involved research, LTSpice simulation, breadboard and oscilloscope testing, iteration and then veroboard soldering and integration. On top of this, I went through a full design flow for creating my own transformer based PSU that outputted a +12V rail, where I drew up and simulated a schematic and then implemented it into PCB form via KiCAD. The PCB was then manufactured, assembled and finally tested against the test bed mains - working successfully.

I also made the final edited showcase video for our project, which I'm very proud of.

NOTE: For the sake of privacy of all members involved in the project, I cannot include the digital logbook which contains the fully documented events of the project, however, I have included all design files revolving around my work on my PSU PCB and the analogue electronics for the microcontroller interface.
